My method

Process before automation.

AI on a broken process only makes it break faster. So every process I touch goes through three questions, always in this order.

01

Keep

What already works?

Protect it. Not everything needs changing, and your team's good habits are an asset, not a problem to fix.

For exampleThe intake call that already wins repeat clients.

02

Systemise

What depends on who does it?

Standardise it: one path, one owner, one template. Quality stops depending on who's in the office that day.

For exampleEvery recruiter submits CVs the same way.

03

Automate

What's repetitive, high-volume and stable?

Only now add automation or AI, with a clear number it has to move. If it can't be measured, it waits.

For exampleThe ATS updates itself after every call.

What founders ask me.

Straight answers.

How is this different from hiring an advisor?

Most advisors hand over a report. I stay through implementation, work with your leadership team rather than around it, and measure every engagement against a real baseline.

Will this disrupt how we work today?

Avoiding that is the point. I build on your current setup and bring in change at a pace your team can absorb without losing momentum.

Do we need AI in every process?

No, and I'll be the first to say so. AI on a broken process makes it break faster. I recommend automation only when the process is solid, the volume justifies it and the outcome is measurable. Some of the biggest wins have nothing to do with AI.

What results can we realistically expect?

Less manual work, faster execution and clear ownership at every stage. We set the baseline on day one, and the first checkpoint is at 30 days.
